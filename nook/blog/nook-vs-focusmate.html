Nook vs Focusmate: the best tools for ADHD body doubling online

Body doubling — the practice of working in the presence of another person — is one of the most effective focus strategies for people with ADHD. Both Nook and Focusmate are built around this idea. Here's how they differ, and which one fits your working style.

What is body doubling, and why does it work?

Body doubling is the practice of working alongside another person — not collaborating, just being present in the same space. For many people with ADHD, the presence of another person activates focus in a way that working alone simply doesn't.

Researchers and clinicians have documented the effect for decades. The working theory: external structure (someone else being "there") compensates for the reduced internal regulation that ADHD can make difficult. You don't need to talk to them. You don't need to be on camera. For many people, even a silent, ambient presence is enough.

"Some of us work better when someone else is in the room. It doesn't matter if they're a stranger."

The good news is that online body doubling works too. Apps like Nook and Focusmate have helped thousands of people with ADHD (and without) build a sustainable focus practice from home.

How Nook works

Nook is a virtual co-working app with named rooms, each with its own ambient sound and timer rhythm. You drop into a room — Beach Café, The Cascade, The Meadow, or After Hours — set a brief intention, and work alongside whoever else is there. No camera. No scheduling. No awkward check-ins.

The rooms run a shared Pomodoro timer so the whole room moves through focus and break cycles together, creating a rhythm without coordination. You can see who else is there (by a name or character they chose), send a quiet "warmth" signal to encourage them, or just be present in silence.

Nook is best for people who want:

  • Ambient presence without video or social pressure
  • Flexibility — start and stop whenever, no booking required
  • A calm aesthetic that doesn't feel like another productivity app
  • Something to run quietly beside them for hours at a time

How Focusmate works

Focusmate pairs you with one other person for a scheduled video session. You book a 25- or 50-minute slot, state your goal at the start, work on camera together, and check in briefly at the end. The accountability is explicit and personal: someone is watching, and you told them what you'd do.

Focusmate is best for people who want:

  • Strong, explicit accountability — a named person expecting you to show up
  • The commitment mechanism of a booked appointment
  • Face-to-face presence, even if silent
  • A fixed session with a clear start and end

Which is better for ADHD?

Both work — and they work through the same underlying mechanism: the presence of another person. The differences are in how much structure they provide and how much friction that structure introduces.

If executive dysfunction is your main challenge

Focusmate's scheduling mechanism can be a superpower: booking a session creates a commitment that makes it harder to defer starting. Many people with ADHD report that they'd never have opened their laptop without a session booked. The video element raises the social stakes further — you told someone you'd be there.

If sensory sensitivity or camera anxiety is a factor

Nook is the better fit. No camera, no obligation to look presentable, no one specific waiting for you. You can drop in mid-thought and leave mid-session without it meaning anything to anyone. The ambient sound (café murmur, rain, waterfall) also helps many ADHD brains reach focus state without the silence feeling oppressive.

If you need something that runs all day

Nook is designed for long-haul use — you can stay in a room for hours, move through multiple Pomodoro cycles, and leave when you're done. Focusmate's session model works best for discrete, bounded tasks rather than a full workday.

If cost or friction is a barrier

Nook is free with no account required to start. You can be in a room in under 10 seconds. Focusmate's free tier limits you to 3 sessions a week; beyond that, a subscription is required.

Choose Nook if…

  • You want no camera, no scheduling
  • You need something to run quietly all day
  • Camera anxiety or sensory sensitivity is a factor
  • You want ambient sound alongside your work
  • You want to try body doubling with zero commitment
  • You work with a team or friend group (private rooms)

Choose Focusmate if…

  • You need strong accountability to start a task
  • A booked appointment helps you follow through
  • Face-to-face presence matters to you
  • You work best in fixed, bounded sessions

Our verdict

They're not really competitors — they sit at different points on the accountability spectrum. Focusmate is high-accountability, high-commitment body doubling. Nook is ambient, low-friction, always-available body doubling.

Many ADHD users use both: Focusmate to tackle the tasks they'd otherwise avoid, and Nook as a background presence for everything else. If you're new to body doubling online and want to try it with zero friction, start with Nook — no sign-up, no booking, open your laptop and you're already with people.

If you've tried ambient approaches and still can't start on your hardest tasks, layer in Focusmate for those specific moments. The two work well together.

Nook is free, requires no account to start, and works on web and Android. Drop into a room and focus alongside real people — right now.
